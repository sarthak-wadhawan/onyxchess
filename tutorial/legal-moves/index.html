Legal move generation

Movement rules answer whether a piece could reach a square. Chess also requires that the moving player’s king remain safe. A move can satisfy the first condition and fail the second.

Pinned pieces and king safety

The white rook on e2 stands between its king and the black rook. Moving it sideways would reveal an attack on the king. Moving along the e-file can keep the line blocked or capture the attacker.

How generate_legal_moves works

  1. Generate all pseudo-legal moves for the active side.
  2. For each candidate, copy the complete board.
  3. Apply the candidate to that copy, including special effects.
  4. Check the king of the player who just moved, not the new active side.
  5. Keep the move only if that king is safe.

Try the rook. The rejected sideways moves never enter the legal list. Our engine does not need a separate pin-detection algorithm: the simulated position exposes the problem.

Getting out of check

When a king is attacked, a legal response must remove every threat. Depending on the position, the player can move the king, block a sliding attack or capture the attacker.

The same copy-and-check function handles these possibilities. For a double check, an ordinary non-king move cannot resolve both attacks. For a knight check, blocking the line is impossible because knights jump.

In this position, White is checked by the rook on e8. Try blocking with the bishop on c3 by moving it to e5. Reset and explore a king escape instead.

Castling: more than a two-square move

Castling moves the king two squares toward its rook and places the rook on the other side of the king. The add_king_moves helper must verify the following before adding a candidate:

  • The relevant castling right remains and both pieces are on their starting squares.
  • Every square between king and rook is empty.
  • The king is not currently in check.
  • Neither the square crossed by the king nor its destination is attacked.

Once the candidate is selected, make_move relocates both pieces and removes the king's castling rights. Try e1–g1, reset, then try e1–c1.

En passant: a capture away from the destination

Black has just pushed d7–d5. White's pawn at e5 can move to d6 and remove the black pawn at d5. The destination itself is empty.

When a pawn double-pushes, make_move saves the square it passed over. The pawn generator checks whether a diagonal destination matches that temporary target and whether the adjacent enemy pawn is present. If so, it records an en passant capture. The move executor removes the pawn from its actual square, not the destination.

The opportunity expires on the next move. Try the capture and observe which square becomes empty.

An en passant edge case

Two pawns can block the same rook attack. An en passant capture removes both from their original rank and may suddenly expose the king. Checking only whether the moving pawn appears pinned would miss this case.

Here, the white king on h5 would be exposed to the rook on a5 if g5 captured f6 en passant. The engine's full-state simulation rejects the capture because the resulting king is attacked. The ordinary forward move is still available.

Promotion: one destination, four choices

When a pawn reaches the opposite back rank, it must become a knight, bishop, rook or queen. The generator records four candidates for the same destination, each with a different promoted piece. A capture and promotion can happen together, so the captured and promoted fields must remain separate.

The player or search chooses one candidate. The move executor replaces the pawn with the chosen piece. Move a7–a8 to see the four options.

When no legal moves remain

Generate moves for the side whose turn it is. If none exist and that king is attacked, the player is checkmated. If none exist but the king is safe, the position is stalemate. The game loop uses the same distinction.

Verify the generator

The starting position has 20 legal moves: 16 pawn pushes and four knight moves. Count positions after two and three plies to check deeper interactions: the expected counts are 400 and 8,902. These counts are useful regression tests; they are not proof that every possible edge case works.
