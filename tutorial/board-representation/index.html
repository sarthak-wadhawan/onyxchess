Board representation

The first problem is not how to play chess. It is how to describe a chess position so that a computer can reason about it. A picture of a board tells us what the position looks like; the engine needs data it can inspect and change.

Represent the pieces

Assign a small integer to every piece. Zero represents an empty square, 1–6 represent white pieces and 7–12 represent black pieces. Both colors use the same ordering: pawn, knight, bishop, rook, queen, king.

Piece White Black
Pawn 1 7
Knight 2 8
Bishop 3 9
Rook 4 10
Queen 5 11
King 6 12

An enumeration lets us give these numbers meaningful names. The piece_color helper identifies which range a number belongs to; piece_type returns the kind of piece, subtracting six for black pieces. Empty squares have no color or piece type. This lets the same movement logic work for both sides.

Represent the squares

Use a one-dimensional array of 64 entries. Number the squares from a1 to h8, moving across each rank before moving up to the next rank.

We need three coordinate helpers:

  • rank_of: divide the index by eight and discard the remainder.
  • file_of: take the remainder after dividing by eight.
  • sq_of: multiply the rank by eight and add the file.

For b2, both internal coordinates are 1, so the index is 9. For h8, both are 7, so the index is 63. Keeping rank and file separate also prevents a move from wrapping around the edge of the board.

Try selecting a1, h1, a2 and h8. Notice how the index changes.

Store the complete position

Pieces alone cannot tell us every legal move. Two visually identical positions can have different castling permissions or en passant opportunities. The Board structure holds the extra information.

Field Why we need it
squares The 64 piece codes
side_to_move The player allowed to act
castling_rights Which of the four castling permissions remain
ep_square_number The temporary en passant destination, or -1
half_move_clock Plies since a pawn move or capture
full_move_number The move number, incremented after Black moves

Four independent castling permissions fit into a bitmask. Assign them values 1, 2, 4 and 8; all four enabled gives 15. Clearing one bit removes just that permission.

Describe an action

The Move structure records the origin, destination, captured piece, promoted piece and special-move flag. Normal moves have flag 0; castling, en passant and promotion use 1, 2 and 3. The record describes what to do, not a complete previous state. When searching, we copy the entire Board before applying a move so that every permission and counter stays independent.

Check your model

Can you determine the index of e4? Can two identical piece arrangements have different legal moves? The answers are 28 and yes: state includes permissions and turn information as well as placement.
